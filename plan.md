# Goal

This project aims to accelerate lattice-field configuration generation using
renormalization-group transformations (RGTs). The current strategy combines
approximate perfect blocking with a conditional normalizing flow for stochastic
inverse blocking. Exact Metropolis–Hastings (MH) updates then remove the
residual bias in the flow-generated configurations.

The framework should eventually generalize to different lattice theories and
symmetry groups, including the two-dimensional (2D) $\phi^4$ theory, the 2D
XY model, 3D $SU(2)$ gauge theory, and ultimately 4D $SU(3)$ gauge theory
and QCD.

## Workflow

1. **Learn an approximate blocking transformation.** Start from fine
   configurations $\phi_f$ on a lattice of linear size $L$. Apply a
   translationally invariant, finite-range, invertible kernel $K$,

   $$
   \psi = K\phi_f,
   $$

   which preserves the number of degrees of freedom. A scale-2 downsampling
   operator $D$ retains one of the four sublattices in 2D (for example, the
   even-even sites), giving

   $$
   \phi_b = D\psi = DK\phi_f.
   $$

   Optimize $K$ so that the blocked ensemble $\{\phi_b\}$ closely matches a
   directly generated coarse ensemble $\{\phi_c\}$ on a lattice of size
   $L/2$. At criticality, treating the coarse action as the same
   parameterized action as the fine action is a practical approximation to a
   fixed-point (perfect-blocking) relation; an exact RG-blocked action is
   generally more complicated.

2. **Parameterize the missing ultraviolet degrees of freedom.** Embed
   $\phi_c$ on the even-even sites of the upscaled lattice. For each
   $2\times2$ block in 2D, introduce three detail fields
   $d_{01},d_{10},d_{11}$, initialized from Gaussian latent variables
   $z$. These fields supply the three degrees of freedom that are absent
   after scale-2 blocking.

3. **Train a conditional upscaling flow.** Train $F_\theta$, conditioned on
   $\phi_c$, to generate the detail fields. A useful staged construction
   generates the two edge sublattices $d_{01}$ and $d_{10}$ before the
   body-centered sublattice $d_{11}$:

   $$
   (\phi_c,z)\xrightarrow{F_\theta}
   (\phi_c,d_{01},d_{10},d_{11})=\widetilde{\psi}.
   $$

   Match local observables of $\widetilde{\psi}$ to those of
   $\psi=K\phi_f$, and include tail-sensitive loss terms to suppress rare,
   large deviations.

4. **Reconstruct an initial fine configuration.** Apply the inverse kernel:

   $$
   \widetilde{\phi}_f
   =K^{-1}\widetilde{\psi}
   =K^{-1}F_\theta(\phi_c,z).
   $$

   The invertibility and numerical conditioning of $K$ must be checked
   explicitly, for example through its Fourier-space spectrum on each
   periodic lattice.

5. **Remove the residual bias with exact updates.** The flow determines only
   the initial state (sweep 0); it does not by itself produce exact samples
   from the fine target distribution. Subsequent coarse and detail updates
   must satisfy detailed balance with respect to the fine action $S_f$.

   If a coarse proposal $\phi_c\to\phi_c'$ is generated by a transition
   kernel reversible with respect to $e^{-S_c}$, accept it with

   $$
   \log A_c
   =-S_f(\phi_f')+S_f(\phi_f)+S_c(\phi_c')-S_c(\phi_c).
   $$

   Update $d_{01}$, $d_{10}$, and $d_{11}$ sequentially. For symmetric
   detail proposals,

   $$
   \log A_d=-S_f(\phi_f')+S_f(\phi_f).
   $$

   In both cases, the acceptance probability is
   $\min(1,e^{\log A})$. Use local patch updates and, on larger lattices, a
   residue-class schedule in which one recorded sweep visits every residue
   class. For a fixed patch size, the work per sweep scales as $O(V)$.
   Multiple chains may be run in parallel, but parallelization is not a
   substitute for the exact MH correction within each chain.

## Design Principles

The kernel $K$ reorganizes the fine degrees of freedom so that the retained
coarse variables carry the long-distance structure, while the complementary
detail variables predominantly describe short-distance fluctuations. This
reduces the burden on the conditional flow, which only needs to reconstruct the
missing ultraviolet modes.

The support of $K$ must be large enough to capture the relevant infrared
structure but small enough to preserve locality and favorable volume scaling.
A five-parameter $5\times5$ kernel is a reasonable baseline, as in the
reference study. In addition to distribution matching, kernel selection should
monitor invertibility, condition number, and stability across lattice sizes.

Approximate blocking is sufficient only if the blocked and directly generated
coarse ensembles overlap well. Any remaining mismatch must be corrected by the
exact coarse and detail MH updates. The blocking kernel is local and may
transfer to larger volumes, whereas the conditional flow need not be
volume-portable; a flow trained for $L=16\to32$ may need to be retrained for
$L=32\to64$.

## 2D $\phi^4$ Theory

The discretized lattice action is

$$
S[\phi]
=-2\kappa\sum_{x,\mu}\phi_x\phi_{x+\hat{\mu}}
+\sum_x\left[\phi_x^2+\lambda\left(\phi_x^2-1\right)^2\right],
$$

where $\phi_x$ is a real scalar field at site $x$, and $\hat{\mu}$ is the
unit vector in direction $\mu$. For the initial 2D study, use
$\lambda=1.0$ and the quoted critical coupling
$\kappa_{\mathrm{cr}}=0.340301$.

Use a set of short- and long-distance observables $\{O_i\}$ to evaluate
overlap between two ensembles. A schematic mean-matching loss is

$$
\mathcal{L}
=\sum_i w_i
\left(\langle O_i\rangle_A-\langle O_i\rangle_B\right)^2,
$$

where the weights should account for the observables' scales and statistical
uncertainties. Prefer a regularized covariance-weighted mismatch,

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cov}}
=\Delta\boldsymbol{\mu}^{\,T}
\left(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}+\epsilon I\right)^{-1}
\Delta\boldsymbol{\mu},
\qquad
\Delta\mu_i=\langle O_i\rangle_A-\langle O_i\rangle_B,
$$

and complement it with distributional or tail-sensitive diagnostics. Agreement
of a finite observable set is evidence of consistency, not proof that two
high-dimensional distributions are identical.

### Configuration Generation

- Generate native $\phi^4$ ensembles using radial updates at
  $L=8,16,32$, initially targeting 1,000 saved configurations per lattice
  size.
- Verify equilibration and estimate integrated autocorrelation times before
  choosing burn-in and saving intervals. Report the effective sample size,
  since 1,000 stored configurations need not be 1,000 independent samples.
- Split statistically independent chains or well-separated configurations into
  training, validation, and test sets.
- Use the pairs $L=8\to16$ and $L=16\to32$ for the initial upscaling study.
  Add native $L=64$ configurations only when testing $L=32\to64$.

### Blocking-Kernel Training

- Begin with a translationally invariant $5\times5$ kernel $K$, choose a
  fixed downsampling operator $D$, and match
  $\{DK\phi_f\}$ to the native coarse ensemble $\{\phi_c\}$.
- Train with covariance-weighted local-observable matching, then validate with
  held-out distributions of the action density, magnetization, two-point
  correlators, susceptibility, correlation length, and Binder cumulant.
- Check the spectrum and condition number of $K$ on every lattice size used
  in training or inference.

### Conditional-Flow Training

- Condition on the embedded coarse field and generate the three detail
  sublattices in stages.
- Match the proposed transformed field
  $F_\theta(\phi_c,z)=\widetilde{\psi}$ to
  $\psi=K\phi_f$ using local-observable and tail-sensitive losses.
- Compare native and initially upscaled fine ensembles on held-out data.
  Monitor distribution overlap rather than only matching means.

### Reconstruction and Exact Sampling

- Construct the initial fine proposal
  $\widetilde{\phi}_f=K^{-1}F_\theta(\phi_c,z)$.
- Rethermalize each proposal with the exact coarse and sequential detail MH
  updates described above.
- Track observables as functions of the recorded sweep number and choose a
  fixed production sweep only after their distributions agree with the native
  fine ensemble within uncertainty.
- Quantify the benefit relative to direct upscaling by measuring acceptance
  rates, rethermalization time, integrated autocorrelation times, effective
  sample size per unit cost, and scaling with volume.

The expected advantage is that the conditional flow learns only the missing
ultraviolet structure rather than the full fine distribution. The approximate
blocking step transfers equilibrated infrared modes to the fine lattice, while
the exact local MH updates correct the remaining bias more efficiently than a
whole-volume accept/reject step.
